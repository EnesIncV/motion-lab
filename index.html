    Learn the physics

    fThe three key formulas
    Range
    R = v²·sin 2θg

    Maximum height
    H = v²·sin²θ2g

    Flight time
    T = 2v·sin θg

    ∠Why 45° flies farthest

    The range depends on sin 2θ. A sine is biggest (exactly 1) at 90°, so 2θ = 90° gives θ = 45°.

    30° and 60° land on the same spot, because sin 60° = sin 120°.

    vVelocity in two parts

    vx = v·cos θ stays the same for the whole flight when there is no air.

    vy = v·sin θ − g·t shrinks by g every second. It is zero at the top, then the ball speeds up downwards.

    ≈Air resistance

    Air pushes against the ball. The push grows with the square of the speed: F ∝ v².

    The path gets shorter and comes down steeper. Every planet uses Earth-like air here, so you can compare. The real Moon has no air at all.

    Learn the physics

    TPeriod of a pendulum
    T = 2π √Lg

    Longer string: slower. Four times longer: two times slower. Stronger gravity: faster.

    Exact for small swings, under about 15°. Big swings take a little longer: compare Formula T with Measured T.

    EEnergy is conserved
    PE = m·g·h    KE = ½·m·v²

    At the top the bob stops for a moment: all potential. At the bottom it is fastest: all kinetic. Without friction KE + PE never changes.

    mWhy mass does not matter

    A heavier bob is pulled harder (F = m·g), but it is also harder to speed up (a = F / m). The mass cancels out:

    a = g·sin θ

    Learn the physics

    rAlways falling, always missing

    The satellite is always falling towards the planet, but it moves sideways so fast that it keeps missing it. Gravity gets weaker with distance: F = G·M·m / r².

    Speed for a circle
    v = √GMr

    Speed to escape
    v = √2GMr

    Slower than the circle speed: an ellipse or a crash. Between the two speeds: an ellipse. Faster than the escape speed: it never comes back.

    How to use Motion Lab

    • Drag from the cannon to aim: the direction sets the angle, the length sets the speed. Let go to fire.
    • Or set the sliders and press Launch (Space).
    • 30° · 45° · 60° fires three balls. Old paths stay on screen in different colours.
    • Air resistance shows the path with air (bright) and without air (faint dots).
    • The target game hides most of the predicted path: use the formula to hit the pad.
    • Drag the bob to a starting angle and let go. With two pendulums, both move together.
    • Change length, gravity, mass and air friction, then compare Formula T with Measured T.
    • The energy bar shows kinetic and potential energy trading places.
    • Drag from the satellite to launch it: the direction sets where it goes, the length sets the speed.
    • Or use the quick buttons: Too slow, Circle, Ellipse, Escape.
    • The dashed line shows the orbit before you launch. Compare your speed with the circle and escape speeds.

    Moving the view

    • Mouse wheel or pinch: zoom. Drag empty space: move the view. Double-click or 0: reset.
